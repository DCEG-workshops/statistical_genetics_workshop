Dr. Jianxin Shi is a senior investigator in the Biostatistics Branch of DCEG. He received his Ph.D. in statistics from Stanford University in 2006, under the supervision of Professor David O. Siegmund. Prior to joining NCI, he spent two years as a postdoctoral research fellow and one year as a research scientist in the Department of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences at Stanford, working on genome-wide association studies of breast cancer, schizophrenia, and depression. Dr. Shi joined the Biostatistics Branch as a tenure-track investigator in 2009 and was appointed senior investigator in 2016.

Dr. Shi is interested in both developing quantitative methods and leading substantive projects to advance knowledge about cancer genomics and its potential translational applications. His research focuses on three areas: cancer genetic epidemiology (identifying genetic variants associated with lung cancer, melanoma, and pancreatic cancer, and developing polygenic risk prediction models for risk stratification); cancer genomics (studying the landscape of lung cancer and chordoma and developing methods for analyzing driver genes, mutual exclusivity, and intratumoral heterogeneity); and microbiome epidemiology (investigating the role of the gut and oral microbiome in cancer etiology).
